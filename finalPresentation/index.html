THE PROBLEM: A DISEASE SILENTLY KILLING SPAIN'S OAK FORESTS

Spanish Dehesa (Extremadura) — ochre soil, sparse isolated oaks
Boxes mark trees suspicious of La Seca — detectable from satellite before visible on the ground

  • The Dehesa — 5 million hectares of oak savanna, SW Spain.
    UNESCO World Heritage landscape
  • La Seca (Phytophthora cinnamomi) kills oak roots silently. Crown damage is visible from satellite before it's visible from the ground.
  • Manual monitoring of 5M hectares is impossible.
  • By the time a forest guard sees a dying tree, it is already too late.
Our goal: Detect which trees show early La Seca symptoms from satellite images — automatically, at scale, with no field visits.
This is not an academic toy problem. Every false negative = a tree that could have been saved.

✓ Phase 1 — Capture
Automated Google Earth scraper. 324 tiles (800×800 px) across the Extremadura Dehesa. Two epochs: Summer 2019 + Winter 2024 on the same GPS coordinates.
✓ Phase 2 — Baseline & Domain Shift
Ran DeepForest (pre-trained on US NEON forests) zero-shot. F1 dropped from 0.68 to 0.32. KS-test (D=0.86) proved statistically the model had never seen soil like this.
✓ Phase 2b — Pre-annotation
Used NEON DeepForest to auto-annotate 324 summer tiles (Pascal VOC XML). Uploaded to Roboflow for human review. Bridges Phase 2 → Phase 3.
✓ Phase 3 — Fine-Tuning
Re-train DeepForest on Dehesa. 30 epochs, RTX 3060. F1-Overall 0.669 (+34.9 pp) but F1-Seca=0.000 — detector can't separate minority class.
✓ Phase 4 — Two-Stage Pipeline
Stage 1: DeepForest detector → boxes. Stage 2: ResNet-18 96px crop classifier. WeightedSampler fixes imbalance. F1-Seca 0.000→0.354 (+35.4 pp).
✓ Phase 5 — End-to-End Evaluation
1-class "Tree" detector fine-tuned + Phase 4 classifier. True pipeline evaluation via IoU≥0.4 matching. F1-Seca 0.347 end-to-end (real system metric).

FINE-TUNING STRATEGY: DOMAIN ADAPTATION WITH CONTROLLED LEARNING

DeepForest — RetinaNet + ResNet-50 backbone
Pre-trained on millions of US NEON forest images. Already knows tree crown shapes, scale variation, and overlapping detection (Focal Loss + FPN).
LR = 1×10−4 — why so low?
High LR destroys the backbone weights before the head can learn — "Catastrophic Forgetting." Low LR preserves what the backbone knows about trees while teaching it Dehesa soil and Seca morphology.
2-class head — original: 1 class ("Tree") → fine-tuned: "Healthy" + "Seca." Head weights re-initialised. Backbone frozen for epoch 1.

TRAINING DYNAMICS: CONVERGENCE WITHOUT OVERFITTING

30 epochs · RTX 3060 · LR = 1×10−4 · Val loss: 1.817 → 0.708 (−61%)

What the curves confirm
Steep drop epochs 14–16: backbone adapts to Dehesa. Val loss tracks train — no overfitting. Still falling at epoch 30 → more epochs would help.
Why Seca F1 = 0.000 despite good loss
87.9% of training labels are Healthy. Gradient from 892 Seca examples is too weak to activate Seca predictions, even with Focal Loss.
Conclusion → Phase 4
The detector learns where trees are (Recall 0.742) but cannot separate classes. Class separation requires the two-stage pipeline.

CRITICAL ANALYSIS: WHERE THE MODEL STILL FAILS

FAILURE 1: Bright Sandy Background

42% of GT boxes missed.
Sparse oaks on ochre soil — low contrast between crown and bright background confuses the anchor grid.

FAILURE 2: High-Density Crown Overlap

56% of GT boxes missed.
Dense canopy — crowns merge into a single connected region, breaking single-tree anchor assumptions.

FAILURE 3: Mixed Shrubs & Shadow

54% of GT boxes missed.
Dense mixed vegetation with shadows — shrubs are confused with tree crowns; shadows shift confidence scores down.

Why these failures matter: All 3 failure modes are systematic, not random noise. They represent distinct visual conditions that need targeted fixes.
Fix paths: (1) bright soil → contrast augmentation, soil-aware normalisation; (2) overlap → NMS tuning or instance segmentation; (3) shadows/shrubs → near-infrared channel, time-of-day normalisation.

CONCLUSION & DEPLOYMENT VISION

✓ What we proved

  • Domain gap between NEON and Dehesa is statistically real (KS D=0.86)
  • Phase 3: fine-tuning raised F1 from 0.32 → 0.669 (+34.9 pp)
  • Phase 4: 2-stage approach broke the Seca barrier — F1-Seca 0.000 → 0.354 (+35.4 pp) on GT crops
  • Phase 5: true end-to-end pipeline — F1-Seca 0.346 on raw tiles; only −0.008 drop vs crop-level, confirming the classifier generalises under real detection noise
  • Ablation confirms low LR is necessary — empirically, not just theoretically
  • Both models public on HuggingFace (sillescas/deepforest-dehesa-quercus)

✗ Remaining limitations

  • F1-Seca 0.354 — good start, needs more Seca annotations to exceed 0.60
  • 3 systematic failure modes: shadow, scale variation, crown overlap
  • RGB only — NIR/SWIR spectral bands would dramatically improve Seca recall
Deployment vision
A regional government forester uploads last summer's Google Earth tiles. The model returns a ranked list of GPS locations where La Seca is likely. Inspectors prioritise those areas. Treatment applied before the tree is lost.

Scale: 5M hectares = ~78,000 tiles of 800px. At ~2 seconds/tile on GPU, a full scan takes under 45 hours — annually.
